# Scroll Storytelling

Use this playbook when the page should unfold as a visual story. Do not impose cinematic scrolling on a simple information page or product workflow.

## Plan the story

- Map the visitor's journey from the opening through the final action. Give each section a reason to exist and a distinct role in the story.
- Vary composition, pacing, and content treatment. Avoid repeating one visual device or turning every section into the same card grid.
- Decide where energy builds, pauses, and resolves. Make the content understandable even when visitors skim or skip sections.
- Treat references as evidence about tone or interaction, not as permission to reproduce a recognizable page.

## Compose with depth

- For a dimensional hero, plan foreground, subject, and background as a coherent scene with readable text and intentional overlap.
- Use supplied photography or original illustration appropriate to the brand. Do not default to the same clay diorama, floating blob, or generic generated landscape.
- Separate motion across layers only when it improves depth or narrative. Keep movement subtle, performant, and stable across scroll positions.
- Create a distinct mobile composition rather than shrinking or cropping the desktop scene without review.
- When reduced motion is requested or enabled, preserve the composition and meaning as a static experience.

## Build and verify

- Prefer semantic sections and progressive enhancement. Avoid scroll hijacking, forced pinning, long unbroken animation chains, and motion that blocks navigation.
- Use native scrolling and performant transform/opacity effects where possible. Avoid excessive listeners and layout-thrashing work.
- Ensure keyboard users can reach content and controls in a sensible order. Never hide essential text behind a scroll or animation trigger.
- Inspect the rendered page at representative scroll positions on desktop and mobile. Verify reduced-motion behavior and the primary action.
- If asset generation or remote services are needed, obtain approval where required and protect source material and credentials.
