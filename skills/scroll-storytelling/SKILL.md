# Scroll Storytelling

Use scrolling to help visitors understand a product in a meaningful order. A page may be compelling without cinematic motion; add scroll choreography only when the brief benefits from it.

## Arrange the explanation

- Decide what a first-time visitor must understand first, what evidence or detail will answer their next question, and where the page should lead them.
- Give each section a distinct informational purpose. A section may demonstrate a capability, explain a tradeoff, show a real use case, or invite an action.
- Base the sequence on verified product details and the user's audience. Do not invent a dramatic story or claim just to create a visual climax.
- Let readers scan, navigate, and skip ahead. Keep headings informative and preserve a sensible reading order.

## Choose presentation, not effects by default

- Vary layout when doing so clarifies the content; do not make every section a card collection or force every section to look different.
- Use imagery, illustration, or depth only when it adds context that copy alone cannot provide. Keep the brand's actual visual character in view.
- Tie animation to a clear event or idea, such as revealing a comparison or showing how a product feature changes state. Avoid motion that merely slows reading.
- Do not hijack scrolling or make essential information depend on pinned scenes, pointer movement, or animation timing.
- Design mobile as its own reading experience. Reposition or simplify visuals when necessary rather than letting a desktop effect dictate the phone layout.

## Make the page dependable

- Ensure content and key actions remain available when animation is disabled or reduced.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; do not replace a complex motion effect with another forced movement.
- Prefer native scrolling and low-cost visual changes. Avoid long chained animations, layout jumps, and effects that make navigation harder.
- When a browser is available, inspect the page from beginning to end on desktop and mobile, including the reduced-motion state. Otherwise report that these visual states were not checked.
